SP-less Triage Loop

Three jobs over service-principal sign-ins whose service principal ID is all zeros. Scoping finds each new app and assigns a lane. Baselining records what the app normally looks like. Alerting re-checks every baselined app for drift, because the ConnectWise case was a known vendor whose stolen credential was used from new infrastructure. Public sources only; describes no particular tenant.

Three jobs, two human gates

JobRunsDoesHuman gate
ScopingDaily, scheduledPulls all-zeros service-principal sign-ins, groups by appId and owner tenant, alerts on any appId not in the kept list. The lookup is one Graph call per owner tenant and can be scripted.Lane assignment. The Microsoft-set test and the exact-domain match are mechanical; "this vendor is ours" and the lane 3 second analyst are not.
BaseliningOn demand, once per laned appIdBaselines 90 days of all that app's sign-ins, zeros or real service principal, and appends one row to the kept list: lane, credential key IDs, resources, ASNs, first seen.Day one only: the sets are reviewed by hand before the row is written, because the first 90 days may already contain the attacker.
AlertingDaily, scheduledReads the kept list, pulls the last two days of sign-ins for every baselined appId, and alerts on any credential key ID, resource or ASN outside the stored sets, and on failures.Case close. An analyst appends the new value to the row. Nothing rebuilds the baseline from raw data, so an attacker's ASN is never baselined the day after first use.

What this loop can and cannot see

  • Scope. The all-zeros filter shows apps that have no service principal in your tenant. A vendor app you consented to has a real one and never appears here. For ConnectWise Cloud Backup that means Scoping sees the exposure in tenants that never used the product; in customer tenants only Alerting, keyed on appId regardless of service principal ID, sees the stolen credential being used from new infrastructure.
  • Prior. Microsoft announced a block on SP-less authentication for non-Microsoft multitenant apps for March 31, 2026, and its FAQ still says tokens are issued "except for a few Microsoft and non-Microsoft applications". A non-Microsoft owner with all-zeros successes after that date is a Microsoft-granted exception. Treat it as rare and high-prior, not routine.
  • No preventive layer. Workload-identity Conditional Access cannot target an app with no service principal. Lanes 1 and 2 are detection only; lane 3 is the only control.
  • Expected gaps. Microsoft's FAQ says some first-party app-only tokens are not published to the service-principal sign-in log, so Graph activity you cannot match to a sign-in is not evidence either way.

Admission rules

LaneEvidence requiredWhat it is not
Lane 1 · MicrosoftOwner tenant is one Microsoft documents, or the appId appears in Microsoft's service principal reference table.Not a tenant whose displayName says Microsoft. Names are free text; a lookup can demote, never promote.
HoldOwner field null; lookup returned nothing (other national cloud, 404); Microsoft-named but undocumented; domain is *.onmicrosoft.com or a lookalike.Not ignored, not disabled. Resolve by appId against Microsoft's table and the community list, or ask Microsoft.
Lane 2 · VendordefaultDomainName exactly equals the vendor's registrable domain, or the appId is one the vendor publishes.Not "a name I recognise". Lane 2 is detection only, so it earns Alerting every day.
Lane 3 · NobodyLookup answered, nobody claims the app, second analyst agrees. Create a service principal for the appId, disable it, verify via Graph that it exists with accountEnabled=false, record its objectId.Not the branch for a failed lookup. Undo is deleting the service principal; re-enabling it leaves a consented app behind.

Day one: the kept list is empty, so every non-Microsoft pair gets the full lookup and a hand review of its Baselining sets before the row is written. Nothing goes to lane 3 on day one.

Queries

Scoping · daily inventory, bucketed in-query


index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-90d@d
    properties.servicePrincipalId="00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000"
| rename properties.appOwnerTenantId as owner_tenant,
    properties.appId as app_id, properties.appDisplayName as app_name
| fillnull value="-" owner_tenant app_name
| eval bucket=case(
    in(owner_tenant, "f8cdef31-a31e-4b4a-93e4-5f571e91255a",
                     "72f988bf-86f1-41af-91ab-2d7cd011db47"), "microsoft",
    owner_tenant="-", "hold: no owner on record",
    true(), "resolve")
| stats count min(_time) as first max(_time) as last
    values(properties.resourceDisplayName) as resources
    by bucket owner_tenant app_id app_name
| sort bucket

Bucket is pre-triage, not the lane. "resolve" rows go through the lookup and the admission rules. Add | where NOT [ inputlookup spless_kept.csv | fields app_id ] on the scheduled run so only unbaselined appIds alert.

Baselining · once per laned appId


index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-90d@d
    properties.appId="<appId being baselined>"
| rename properties.appId as app_id,
    properties.servicePrincipalCredentialKeyId as key_id,
    properties.resourceDisplayName as resource,
    properties.autonomousSystemNumber as asn
| fillnull value="-" key_id resource asn
| stats values(key_id) as key_ids values(resource) as resources
    values(asn) as asns min(_time) as first_seen by app_id
| eval owner_tenant="<owner tenant>", lane="<1|hold|2|3>",
    key_ids=mvjoin(key_ids,"|"), resources=mvjoin(resources,"|"),
    asns=mvjoin(asns,"|")
| outputlookup append=true spless_kept.csv

No zeros filter: the baseline covers the app under a real service principal too. Review the sets by hand before writing on day one; that review is where an attacker already in the 90 days gets caught.

Alerting · daily drift on baselined appIds


index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-2d@d
    [ inputlookup spless_kept.csv | fields app_id | rename app_id as properties.appId ]
| rename properties.appId as app_id,
    properties.servicePrincipalCredentialKeyId as key_id,
    properties.resourceDisplayName as resource,
    properties.autonomousSystemNumber as asn, resultType as result
| fillnull value="-" key_id resource asn result
| dedup properties.id
| stats count by app_id key_id resource asn result
| lookup spless_kept.csv app_id OUTPUT key_ids resources asns
| eval why=mvappend(
    if(isnull(mvfind(split(key_ids,"|"), "^".key_id."$")), "new keyId", null()),
    if(isnull(mvfind(split(asns,"|"),    "^".asn."$")),    "new ASN",   null()),
    if(isnull(mvfind(split(resources,"|"),"^".resource."$")),"new resource", null()),
    if(result!="0", "failure ".result, null()))
| where isnotnull(why)
| table app_id why key_id resource asn result count

The kept list stores each set pipe-delimited. No success filter: a failed sign-in for a baselined vendor appId from a new ASN is the signal, and error-code shifts (invalid or expired secret) mean someone is testing credentials. Two-day window with dedupe covers export lag. Same cloud provider as the vendor is not clearance.

Microsoft's own inventory call, from its reports FAQ: GET https://graph.microsoft.com/beta/auditLogs/signIns?$filter=signInEventTypes/any(t: t eq 'servicePrincipal') and servicePrincipalId eq '00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000'. Beta is required; v1.0 returns no service-principal sign-ins and no owner field. Field names above follow the Azure Monitor export shape; the Log Analytics columns are documented, the exact event-hub keys are inferred from them, so tune to your add-on.

Prerequisites

  • Lookup: CrossTenantInformation.ReadBasic.All; no directory role required.
  • Lane 3: Application Administrator or Cloud Application Administrator; New-MgServicePrincipal -AppId needs Application.ReadWrite.All. The creation emits an "Add service principal" audit event; allow-list the loop's own creations in consent alerting.
  • Retention: 90 days where your export keeps it; the Entra portal keeps 30.
  • Never apply lane 3 to anything that appears in the preview MicrosoftServicePrincipalSignInLogs stream; Microsoft warns that disabling those can lock a tenant out.
