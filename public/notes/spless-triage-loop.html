SP-less Triage Loop

Four jobs over service-principal sign-ins whose service principal ID is all zeros. Scoping finds each new app and assigns a lane. Baselining records what the app normally looks like. Alerting re-checks every baselined app for drift, because the ConnectWise case was a known vendor whose stolen credential was used from new infrastructure. Investigating is where anything that fails a review goes: nothing is written to the kept list until it comes back benign. Public sources only; describes no particular tenant.

Four jobs, three human gates

JobRunsDoesHuman gate
ScopingDaily, scheduledPulls all-zeros service-principal sign-ins, groups by appId and owner tenant, alerts on any appId not in the kept list. The lookup is one Graph call per owner tenant and can be scripted.Lane assignment. The Microsoft-set test and the exact-domain match are mechanical; "this vendor is ours" and the lane 3 second analyst are not.
BaseliningOn demand, once per laned appIdBaselines 90 days of all that app's sign-ins, zeros or real service principal, and appends one row to the kept list: lane, credential key IDs, resources, ASNs, first seen.Every new app: the sets are reviewed by hand before the row is written, because the 90 days behind a newly seen app may already contain the attacker. Not clean → Investigating; the row is not written. Day one is only the day there are many.
AlertingDaily, scheduledReads the kept list, pulls the last two days of sign-ins for every baselined appId, and alerts on any credential key ID, resource or ASN outside the stored sets, and on failures.None. Anything new → Investigating. Alerting never writes the kept list.
InvestigatingOn demand, from either exitIssuance-then-use (did the all-zeros token get used: 2xx Graph activity from the same appId), persistence audit (roles, credentials, add→remove→add churn), blast radius (resources, mailboxes, sibling principals touched). The three queries and the containment steps are below.The verdict. Benign: write the row or append the new value, by hand. Malicious: IR case, contain (create-then-disable the service principal, revoke what it touched), mark the row compromised and never append. Nothing rebuilds the baseline from raw data, so an attacker's ASN is never baselined the day after first use.

What this loop can and cannot see

  • Scope. The all-zeros filter shows apps that have no service principal in your tenant. A vendor app you consented to has a real one and never appears here. For ConnectWise Cloud Backup that means Scoping sees the exposure in tenants that never used the product; in customer tenants only Alerting, keyed on appId regardless of service principal ID, sees the stolen credential being used from new infrastructure.
  • Prior. Microsoft announced a block on SP-less authentication for non-Microsoft multitenant apps for March 31, 2026, and its FAQ still says tokens are issued "except for a few Microsoft and non-Microsoft applications". A non-Microsoft owner with all-zeros successes after that date is a Microsoft-granted exception. Treat it as rare and high-prior, not routine.
  • No preventive layer. Workload-identity Conditional Access cannot target an app with no service principal. Lanes 1 and 2 are detection only; lane 3 is the only control.
  • Expected gaps. Microsoft's FAQ says some first-party app-only tokens are not published to the service-principal sign-in log, so Graph activity you cannot match to a sign-in is not evidence either way.

Every newly laned app gets the full lookup and a hand review of its Baselining sets before the row is written. Day one is the same procedure at volume, with one extra rule: nothing goes to lane 3 on day one.

Queries

Scoping · daily inventory, bucketed in-query


index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-90d@d
    properties.servicePrincipalId="00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000"
| rename properties.appOwnerTenantId as owner_tenant,
    properties.appId as app_id, properties.appDisplayName as app_name
| fillnull value="-" owner_tenant app_name
| eval bucket=case(
    in(owner_tenant, "f8cdef31-a31e-4b4a-93e4-5f571e91255a",
                     "72f988bf-86f1-41af-91ab-2d7cd011db47"), "microsoft",
    owner_tenant="-", "hold: no owner on record",
    true(), "resolve")
| stats count min(_time) as first max(_time) as last
    values(properties.resourceDisplayName) as resources
    by bucket owner_tenant app_id app_name
| sort bucket

Bucket is pre-triage, not the lane. "resolve" rows go through the lookup and the admission rules. Add | where NOT [ inputlookup spless_kept.csv | fields app_id ] on the scheduled run so only unbaselined appIds alert.

Baselining · once per laned appId


index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-90d@d
    properties.appId="<appId being baselined>"
| rename properties.appId as app_id,
    properties.servicePrincipalCredentialKeyId as key_id,
    properties.resourceDisplayName as resource,
    properties.autonomousSystemNumber as asn
| fillnull value="-" key_id resource asn
| stats values(key_id) as key_ids values(resource) as resources
    values(asn) as asns min(_time) as first_seen by app_id
| eval owner_tenant="<owner tenant>", lane="<1|hold|2|3>",
    key_ids=mvjoin(key_ids,"|"), resources=mvjoin(resources,"|"),
    asns=mvjoin(asns,"|")
| outputlookup append=true spless_kept.csv

No zeros filter: the baseline covers the app under a real service principal too. Review the sets by hand before writing, for every new app; that review is where an attacker already in the 90 days gets caught.

Alerting · daily drift on baselined appIds


index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-2d@d
    [ inputlookup spless_kept.csv | fields app_id | rename app_id as properties.appId ]
| rename properties.appId as app_id,
    properties.servicePrincipalCredentialKeyId as key_id,
    properties.resourceDisplayName as resource,
    properties.autonomousSystemNumber as asn, resultType as result
| fillnull value="-" key_id resource asn result
| dedup properties.id
| stats count by app_id key_id resource asn result
| lookup spless_kept.csv app_id OUTPUT key_ids resources asns
| eval why=mvappend(
    if(isnull(mvfind(split(key_ids,"|"), "^".key_id."$")), "new keyId", null()),
    if(isnull(mvfind(split(asns,"|"),    "^".asn."$")),    "new ASN",   null()),
    if(isnull(mvfind(split(resources,"|"),"^".resource."$")),"new resource", null()),
    if(result!="0", "failure ".result, null()))
| where isnotnull(why)
| table app_id why key_id resource asn result count

The kept list stores each set pipe-delimited. No success filter: a failed sign-in for a baselined vendor appId from a new ASN is the signal, and error-code shifts (invalid or expired secret) mean someone is testing credentials. Two-day window with dedupe covers export lag. Same cloud provider as the vendor is not clearance.

Microsoft's own inventory call, from its reports FAQ: GET https://graph.microsoft.com/beta/auditLogs/signIns?$filter=signInEventTypes/any(t: t eq 'servicePrincipal') and servicePrincipalId eq '00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000'. Beta is required; v1.0 returns no service-principal sign-ins and no owner field. Field names above follow the Azure Monitor export shape; the Log Analytics columns are documented, the exact event-hub keys are inferred from them, so tune to your add-on.

Investigating · queries for one appId

Issuance-then-use


index=* category=MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs earliest=-30d@d
    properties.appId="<appId under investigation>"
| rename properties.responseStatusCode as code, properties.requestUri as uri,
    properties.requestMethod as method
| where tonumber(code)>=200 AND tonumber(code)<300
| stats count as ok2xx values(method) as methods values(uri) as uris
    min(_time) as first max(_time) as last by properties.appId
→ any row = the app acted despite "no consent"; writes (POST/PATCH/DELETE)
  against roles, servicePrincipals or applications go straight to the verdict

Prerequisite: Microsoft Graph activity logs must be enabled. Off means a coverage gap, not a clean result. First-party app-only tokens are not in the sign-in log, so Graph rows you cannot pair with a sign-in are not evidence either way.

Persistence audit


# two views of one appId: what it DID (actor) and what was done TO it (target)
index=* category=AuditLogs earliest=-30d@d ("<appId>" OR "<app display name>")
    operationName IN ("Add member to role", "Remove member from role",
        "Add app role assignment to service principal",
        "Remove app role assignment from service principal",
        "Add service principal credentials", "Remove service principal credentials",
        "Update application – Certificates and secrets management",
        "Add service principal", "Remove service principal", "Delete application",
        "Add owner to application", "Add owner to service principal",
        "Consent to application", "Add delegated permission grant")
| rename properties.initiatedBy.app.appId as actor_app,
    properties.initiatedBy.app.displayName as actor_name,
    properties.initiatedBy.user.userPrincipalName as actor_user,
    properties.targetResources{}.displayName as target, properties.result as result
| eval side=if(actor_app="<appId>", "actor", "target")
| table _time side operationName actor_app actor_name actor_user target result
| sort _time

# churn: the same target gets an add AND a remove inside 60 min (operator or agent at the keyboard)
index=* category=AuditLogs earliest=-30d@d ("<appId>" OR "<app display name>")
    operationName IN ("Add member to role", "Remove member from role",
        "Add service principal credentials", "Remove service principal credentials")
| rename properties.targetResources{}.displayName as target
| eval verb=if(match(operationName, "^Add"), "add", "remove")
| transaction target maxspan=60m
| where mvcount(mvdedup(verb))=2
| table _time duration target operationName
→ actor rows: the app changed something (self-assigned a role, put a credential on another app)
→ target rows: someone changed the app (a credential added since the vendor's incident date is the way in)
→ sibling principals it touched go on the containment list

Blast radius


# 1) resources the token was minted for, and from where
index=* category=ServicePrincipalSignInLogs earliest=-30d@d properties.appId="<appId>"
| stats count min(_time) as first max(_time) as last
    by properties.resourceDisplayName properties.ipAddress properties.autonomousSystemNumber

# 2) Graph: which object families, how many distinct users
index=* category=MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs earliest=-30d@d properties.appId="<appId>"
| rename properties.requestUri as uri, properties.requestMethod as method,
    properties.responseStatusCode as code
| eval family=case(match(uri,"/messages|/mailFolders"), "mail",
    match(uri,"/drives|/sites"), "files", match(uri,"/directoryRoles|/roleManagement"), "roles",
    match(uri,"/servicePrincipals|/applications|/oauth2PermissionGrants"), "apps",
    match(uri,"/users"), "users", match(uri,"/groups"), "groups", true(), "other")
| rex field=uri "/users/(?<user>[^/?]+)"
| stats count dc(user) as users values(method) as methods by family code
| sort family

# 3) mailboxes actually read (Microsoft 365 unified audit log; MailItemsAccessed needs the
#    auditing license or the mailbox audit setting enabled — if absent, that is a gap, not a zero)
index=* Workload=Exchange Operation=MailItemsAccessed AppId="<appId>" earliest=-30d@d
| stats count dc(MailboxOwnerUPN) as mailboxes values(ClientIPAddress) as ips by AppId
→ the "users", "mail" and "files" rows and the mailbox count are the notification scope

Contain

  1. Stop new tokens. If the app has a service principal in your tenant, disable it (accountEnabled=false). Disable, do not delete: deletion destroys the object the audit trail points at. If it has none, create one for the appId and disable it, which is Microsoft's documented block for SP-less apps.
  2. Accept the token tail. App-only access tokens cannot be revoked; they expire on their own, 60 to 90 minutes by Microsoft's default lifetime. Disabling the service principal stops issuance, so the exposure window closes within that span. Watch the Graph activity log for the appId until it goes quiet.
  3. Undo what it did. From the persistence audit's actor rows: remove role assignments it granted (Global Administrator first), delete credentials it added to other applications or service principals, delete sibling service principals it created, revoke consent grants it added. Each of these is its own audit event, so the undo is itself auditable.
  4. Close the way in. From the target rows: remove any credential added to the app since the vendor's incident date. If the vendor holds the credential, the vendor rotates it; keep the service principal disabled until the vendor confirms.
  5. Users it reached. For every user in the blast radius, revoke sign-in sessions (revokeSignInSessions), review inbox rules and mail forwarding, and reset credentials where the app had write scope on the user.
  6. Put a control in front of it. With a service principal now present, workload-identity Conditional Access can target the app: block it except from the vendor's known locations before it is ever re-enabled.
  7. Preserve, then record. Export the sign-in, audit and Graph activity logs for the window before portal retention (30 days) drops them. Mark the kept-list row compromised with the case ID; it is never appended.

Containment order follows Microsoft's Storm-0940 post (disable the application identities, review credentials and role assignments, revoke sessions) and Huntress's guidance to disable rather than delete. Undo for a lane 3 block is deleting the created service principal; undo for containment is the vendor's confirmed rotation plus a permission review, never a bare re-enable.

Prerequisites

  • Lookup: CrossTenantInformation.ReadBasic.All; no directory role required.
  • Lane 3: Application Administrator or Cloud Application Administrator; New-MgServicePrincipal -AppId needs Application.ReadWrite.All. The creation emits an "Add service principal" audit event; allow-list the loop's own creations in consent alerting.
  • Retention: 90 days where your export keeps it; the Entra portal keeps 30.
  • Never apply lane 3 to anything that appears in the preview MicrosoftServicePrincipalSignInLogs stream; Microsoft warns that disabling those can lock a tenant out.
